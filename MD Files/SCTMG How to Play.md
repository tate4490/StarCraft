# SCTMG How to Play

A step-by-step guide to playing a game of StarCraft: Tabletop Miniature Game, from the draft to the final score. This file gives the order of play and the rules that apply at each step. Detailed rules for measuring, line of sight, supply and special abilities are in the files listed in Section 10.

Before you start, build your army using `SCTMG Army Building.md`.

---

## 1. The Game in Brief

Each player is the general of an army. Before the game begins, you build that army by spending resources to recruit Units of Archon Studio miniatures, then wage war against your opponent.

A game lasts up to 5 Rounds. Each Round has four Phases, resolved in strict order:

1. **Movement Phase.** Deploy, Move, Disengage, or Hold.
2. **Assault Phase.** Ranged Attack, Charge, Run, or Hold.
3. **Combat Phase.** Close Combat Attack for every Engaged Unit.
4. **Scoring and Cleanup Phase.** Control Mission Markers, score Victory Points, check for the end of the game, resolve end-of-round effects, clean up, and set initiative.

Victory Points (VP) decide the winner. Mission Markers are the main way to score them.

---

## 2. Before the Game

### 2.1 Build the Armies

Each player builds an army using the rules in `SCTMG Army Building.md`. Tactical Cards and the Faction Card are set face-up on the table before the game starts.

### 2.2 Choose the Mission and Deployment

Each player brings 2 Mission Cards and 2 Deployment Cards to the table. Players then run a short draft to choose one of each. The draft ensures neither player enters with a pre-arranged advantage.

**Step 1: Roll-Off.** Both players roll off (2D6, higher total wins; ties are re-rolled). The winner makes two choices, in order:

- **Colour:** Declare yourself the Red Player or the Blue Player. This determines your Entry Edge, your Zone of Influence, and your Mission Marker affinity for the whole game.
- **Draft control:** Choose to control either the Mission Draft or the Deployment Draft. Your opponent automatically controls the other.

**Step 2: Mission Draft.** The player without Mission Draft control removes any 2 Mission Cards from the table. The player with Mission Draft control selects 1 of the 2 remaining cards. This becomes the Mission for the game. Discard the last card.

**Step 3: Deployment Draft.** Repeat the same process for the Deployment Cards. The player without Deployment Draft control removes any 2 Deployment Cards. The player with Deployment Draft control selects 1 of the 2 remaining cards. This becomes the Deployment for the game. Discard the last card.

**Step 4: Marker Affinity.** Once both cards are chosen, assign Mission Marker affinity by number:

- Markers 1 and 3 are associated with the Red Player.
- Markers 2 and 4 are associated with the Blue Player.
- Marker 5 begins Neutral.

Affinity shows which player’s colour is printed on each Marker. It does not grant starting control.

### 2.3 Mission and Deployment Card Details

A **Mission Card** defines the game’s victory conditions and pacing. It gives the timing windows for triggered rules, the Starting Supply and Supply Escalation values, the game length (usually 5 Rounds), and any Additional Conditions such as instant-win scenarios.

A **Deployment Card** defines the battlefield’s physical layout. It gives the battlefield dimensions, the coordinates for Mission Markers 1 to 5, the Entry Edge for each player, and the Zone of Influence, which extends 6” inward from each player’s Entry Edge.

### 2.4 Set Up the Battlefield

Set up the battlefield in this order:

1. Confirm the table dimensions shown on the Deployment Card.
2. Assign Entry Edges to each player as shown on the Deployment Card.
3. Set Zone of Influence Markers at the corners of each player’s Entry Edge, where the Entry Edge does not run the full length of a table edge. Place them exactly as the Deployment Card instructs.
4. Set terrain. Either select or roll for one of the pre-made maps at the back of the rulebook (roll a D6 for Standard Engagement, or a D3 for Skirmish), or starting with the Red Player, players alternate placing one terrain piece at a time, following the terrain density guidelines below.
5. Place Mission Markers at the coordinates listed on the Deployment Card. Markers can be set on Mid Ground and High Ground. They cannot be placed on impassable terrain. If a Marker’s coordinates would overlap impassable terrain, move that terrain piece as close as possible to the Marker without overlapping it.

**Terrain density guidelines** (for a Standard 36” × 54” table; scale proportionally for other sizes). Recommended total is 8 to 12 pieces:

- 0 to 2 pieces of Size 0 (scatter terrain).
- 2 to 4 pieces of Size 1 (barricades, low walls, scatter crates).
- 6 to 8 pieces of Size 2 (ruins, bunkers, wrecked vehicles).
- If available, 1 to 2 pieces of Size 3 or larger, which create High Ground.
- 4 to 6 pieces of Grass (Size 2 special).

Divide the table into four quarters. Each quarter should contain at least 2 significant pieces (Size 2 or larger). No quarter should be empty, and none should be so dense that Units cannot manoeuvre through it. The area within 6” of the table centre should contain at least one significant terrain piece. Leave at least 2 clear fire lanes of 6” or wider, running roughly from one Entry Edge towards the other. Every piece of Size 3 or larger terrain that models can stand on should have at least one ACCESS POINT reachable from Ground Level without passing through another Size 2+ terrain piece.

### 2.5 Pre-Game Protocol

1. Select a Mission Card and a Deployment Card.
2. Set Mission Markers at the coordinates on the Deployment Card.
3. Set terrain.
4. Roll off. The winner assigns the First Player Marker for Round 1.

## 3. The Round

### 3.1 Round Sequence

1. **Start of the Round.** Resolve all “Start of the Round” abilities. If both players have effects, the First Player Marker holder resolves theirs first. Each player with several such effects chooses the order, and each effect fully resolves before the next begins. At the start of each Round, all Units gain the STATIONARY Status, and BURROWED Units gain HIDDEN.
2. **Phase 1: Movement.**
3. **Phase 2: Assault.**
4. **Phase 3: Combat.**
5. **Phase 4: Scoring and Cleanup.**

### 3.2 Supply Pool

At the start of the game, the Supply Pool equals the Starting Supply on the Mission Card. It increases by the Supply Escalation value at the start of each later Round. Total Current Supply on the battlefield can never exceed the Supply Pool. In the final Round (set by the Mission Card), the Supply Pool is unlimited.

Available Supply equals the Supply Pool minus the Total Current Supply of Friendly Units on the battlefield. A Unit can only be deployed or summoned if its Current Supply Value is less than or equal to Available Supply. See `SCTMG Supply System.md` for details (file to be created).

### 3.3 Activations and Initiative

During Phases 1, 2 and 3, players use **Alternating Activation**:

- The holder of the First Player Marker decides which player activates first in each Phase.
- Players then alternate, each activating one Unit per turn.
- Each activated Unit performs exactly one action from the list available in that Phase. After the action, the Unit gets an Activation Marker.

**Passing.** On your turn, you may Pass instead of activating a Unit. You must Pass if you have no eligible Units left.

- The first player to Pass in a Phase takes the First Player Marker for the next Phase.
- Once a player Passes, they cannot activate any further Units that Phase. The opponent completes their remaining activations.
- Set Activation Markers on any Friendly Units on the battlefield that did not activate.

**Round 1 first player.** Players roll off. The winner assigns the First Player Marker to either player.

### 3.4 Units

A Unit is one or more models acting together. Each Unit is in one of these states at any time:

- **In Reserves.** Not on the battlefield. Units start the game here.
- **Unengaged.** On the battlefield and not in close combat.
- **Engaged.** On the battlefield and in close combat with an Enemy Ground Unit (see Section 7).
- **Destroyed.** All its models have been removed. A Destroyed Unit does not return to play.

Models are measured through their bases only. See `SCTMG Core Concepts.md`.

## 4. Phase 1: Movement

### 4.1 Starting the Phase

At the start of the Movement Phase, verify Available Supply for all players.

### 4.2 Activations

On your turn, choose one:

- **Activate a Unit on the battlefield** that has no Activation Marker. Perform a Move, Hold, or Disengage action.
- **Activate a Unit in Reserves** that has no Activation Marker. Perform a Deploy action to bring it onto the battlefield.

After the action, set a Movement-side Activation Marker next to the Unit.

### 4.3 Hold

The Unit performs no action. Set an Activation Marker beside it. It counts as Activated for the Phase.

### 4.4 Move

An Unengaged Unit changes its position on the battlefield. Resolve a Move as follows.

**Step 1: The Leading Model.**

- Choose a Leading Model and move it up to the Unit’s Speed. Measure the actual path travelled. Units with a split Speed value use the second value only when reduced to a single remaining model, or when the Unit started with a single model.
- The Leading Model may pass through Friendly models in its own Unit.
- It may not pass through Enemy models or Friendly models from other Units. Ground models may pass through a Flying model’s base as if it were not there, and vice versa.
- It cannot move through terrain of Size 2 or larger.
- It must satisfy Gap Clearance (Section 4.7).
- It cannot change elevation unless it passes through an ACCESS POINT.
- It may not end overlapping terrain or another model’s base, and its entire base must end on the battlefield.
- It cannot end within 1” (Engagement Range) of an Enemy Ground Unit.
- No part of its base can move more than the Unit’s Speed.

**Step 2: The Other Models.**

- Pick up the remaining models and set them in Coherency around the Leading Model (see Section 4.8).
- They are set, not moved. They may ignore intervening obstacles.
- They must still end in a legal position: no overlapping bases, entire bases on the battlefield, and not within 1” of an Enemy Unit.

### 4.5 Terrain Interactions

- **Size 0 and Size 1:** Pass through freely. A model cannot end movement overlapping the terrain’s footprint.
- **Grass (Size 2, special):** Grass does not block movement but does block Line of Sight. If a Leading Model’s path passes through a Grass piece, or any model of a Unit ends on one, that Grass piece is removed from the game for the rest of the battle. Flying Units pass over Grass without destroying it, but if a Flying model ends on Grass, it is removed as normal.
- **Size 2 and larger:** Cannot be moved through.

**Elevation.** Models stand at one of three elevations: Ground Level (on the playmat), Mid Ground (on horizontal terrain of Size 1 or 2), or High Ground (on horizontal terrain of Size 3 or larger). A model on several levels is on the highest one. Models change elevation only by passing through an ACCESS POINT (for example, a ladder, portal, or door). Ramps are Size 1 Mid Ground terrain. Models may only enter and exit a ramp through its two ACCESS POINTS, but may end their movement on it.

### 4.6 Flying Move

Units with the Flying Combat Tag ignore all normal movement restrictions. The Leading Model moves point-to-point, ignoring all terrain and models between the start and end. Other models may pass through a Flying model’s base as if it were not there.

A Flying Unit may end its movement in base-to-base contact with Ground Units, and vice versa. A Flying Unit must end its movement at least 1” away from all Enemy Flying Units.

### 4.7 Gap Clearance

The Leading Model can pass through gaps depending on the Unit’s Size:

- Size 2 or lower: may pass through gaps at least 1” wide.
- Size 3 or larger: requires a gap at least 3” wide.

A model cannot be set where its base does not physically fit, whatever the gap width. Gap Clearance applies to Move, Run, Charge, Disengage, and Close Ranks. Flying Units ignore it, but their Leading Model must still end in a legal position.

### 4.8 Coherency

Coherency is checked at the end of any action that repositions models, including Move, Deploy, Charge, Disengage, Close Ranks, Run, PLACE, and SUMMON.

At the end of such an action, every model in the Unit must be Wholly Within 3” of the Leading Model and able to trace a valid Coherency Link to it. A Coherency Link is a chain of imaginary lines drawn model-to-model through models of the same Unit. Links cannot pass through models from other Units, terrain, or gaps that the Leading Model could not move through. A link may pass through Enemy models the Unit is Engaged with, and through ACCESS POINTS. Links for Flying Units ignore terrain and may pass through models from other Units.

If a model cannot be placed in a legal position while keeping a valid Coherency Link, it is removed as a casualty immediately. A Unit that is not In Coherency cannot control or contest Mission Markers. A Unit remains In Coherency until its next repositioning action.

### 4.9 Disengage

An Engaged Unit withdraws from melee.

- Move it using the standard Move rules.
- Any individual model that cannot end its movement strictly outside the Engagement Range of all Enemy Units is immediately removed and considered Destroyed. Other models that clear the Engagement Range remain unaffected.
- If the Leading Model itself cannot end outside the Engagement Range of all Enemy Units, the Disengage fails. The Unit does not move, the Leading Model is removed, and the activation ends.
- A Unit that Disengages cannot perform a Ranged Attack or Charge in the following Assault Phase, unless its Current Supply Value is greater than the Combined Current Supply Value of all Enemy Units it was Engaged with (Tactical Mass).

### 4.10 Deploy

A Unit from Reserves enters the battlefield.

- Confirm the Unit’s Current Supply Value fits within Available Supply.
- Treat the Entry Edge as the base of a Leading Model placed outside the battlefield. Move the Leading Model up to the Unit’s Speed using the standard Move rules.
- The Leading Model enters first. Set the rest of the Unit in Coherency.
- The Unit cannot finish its activation within the Enemy’s Zone of Influence.
- A Unit can never end a Deploy action Out of Coherency. If all models cannot be placed legally while keeping Coherency, the Unit cannot be deployed in that location.

Units in Reserves cannot use their abilities. When a Unit is returned to Reserves by a rule, it releases its Current Supply and keeps its equipment and any Damage Markers. Tokens and Markers it left behind are removed unless they have STAY IN PLAY. If the Unit was Activated this Phase, it stays Activated.

## 5. Phase 2: Assault

### 5.1 Activations

On your turn, activate a Unit with a Movement-side Activation Marker. Select one action: Run, Hold, Charge, or Ranged Attack. After completing the action, set an Assault-side Activation Marker next to the Unit. Units still in Reserves cannot act in this Phase.

### 5.2 Passing

The first player to Pass takes the First Player Marker for Phase 3. Set Assault-side Activation Markers on any Units that did not activate.

### 5.3 Hold

The Unit does not take any action. Set an Assault-side Activation Marker.

### 5.4 Run

Run lets a Unit reposition without fighting. An Unengaged Unit moves across the battlefield using the standard Move rules (Section 4.4), including Gap Clearance and the restriction on ending within the Engagement Range of Enemy Units.

### 5.5 Ranged Attack

A Ranged Attack is resolved in two stages. First, the attacking player selects a valid target and assigns weapons to it. Second, the attack is resolved through the three-pool system (Section 5.7).

**Target eligibility.** A valid target must meet all of these:

- At least one Visible model from the target Unit is within the weapon’s maximum Range. Measure from the attacking model’s base to the target model’s base.
- Only models within Range of at least one Visible model from the target Unit contribute dice.
- The target’s Combat Tag matches the weapon’s target characteristic (Ground, Flying, or All).
- Engagement status: an Unengaged attacking Unit cannot select an Engaged Unit as a target (except Spillover from Template Weapons, Section 5.9). An Engaged attacking Unit may only target the Unit(s) it is currently Engaged with.

**Weapon selection and batching.** Each model fires one of its available Assault Phase weapons. All models using the same weapon profile must fire at the same target. A **Batch** is all models firing the same weapon at the same target. The same rule applies to SIDEARM weapons, which may target a different Enemy Unit from the Unit’s other weapons. A Unit with several weapon profiles may fire at different targets, resolved as separate Batches.

- When a Special Ability reduces the number of dice in a Batch, the controlling player of the ability chooses which dice are removed.
- Declare the target of one Batch, resolve it fully, and then declare the next Batch. Targets are not all declared at once.

### 5.6 Charge

An Unengaged Ground Unit moves into the Engagement Range of Enemy Units.

1. **Declare targets.** All targets must have the Ground Combat Tag. Flying Units cannot Charge or be Charged. A Charge does not require Line of Sight. Declare all intended targets before rolling.
2. **Determine Charge Distance.** Choose the Leading Model and nominate one model per target Unit as the Charge target. Roll 1D6 and add it to the Unit’s Speed. This is the Charge Roll Distance. Measure along the actual path of travel, bypassing impassable obstacles and following Gap Clearance.
3. **Check success.** The Charge succeeds only if the Leading Model can be legally set in a position that is:
   - Wholly Within the Charge Roll Distance of its starting point.
   - Within the Engagement Range (1”) of every declared Charge target.
   - Not overlapping other bases or impassable terrain.
   - Not within the Engagement Range of any Enemy Unit that was not declared as a target.

   If the Charge fails, the Unit does not move and its activation ends.
4. **Execute the move.** Move the Leading Model into position, in base-to-base contact with the Charge target(s) if possible. Set the remaining models in Coherency, using this placement priority:
   - Priority A: base-to-base contact with declared target models, where physically possible.
   - Priority B: within the Engagement Range of the targets, if base-to-base is impossible.
   - Priority C: as close as possible to the Leading Model to maintain Coherency.

   None may end within the Engagement Range of a Unit not declared as a target.

Charges that succeed may trigger IMPACT hits (see `SCTMG Keywords.md`).

### 5.7 Ranged Attack Resolution

Dice begin in the **Attack Pool**, pass through the **Armour Pool**, and end in the **Damage Pool**. Dice that survive the whole sequence inflict casualties. Resolve each Batch in this order.

1. **Roll to Hit.** For each attacking model with an eligible target, add dice equal to its weapon’s Rate of Attack (RoA) to the Attack Pool. If the weapon has S Dice, add one Surge Die in a different colour. If there are no S Dice, the Surge Result is 0. Roll all Attack Dice. Results equal to or higher than the weapon’s Hit value are successes and move to the Armour Pool. Discard failures.
2. **Resolve Surge.** Check whether the target Unit’s Combat Tag matches the weapon’s Surge Type. If not, discard the Surge Die result. If it matches, move a number of dice equal to the Surge Die result from the Armour Pool directly to the Damage Pool. These dice bypass Armour. Surge can never move more dice than are in the Armour Pool.
3. **Armour Rolls.** The defender rolls all dice in the Armour Pool. Results equal to or higher than the Unit’s Armour characteristic are successes and are discarded. Lower results are failures and move to the Damage Pool.
4. **Evade Rolls.** Only if the target qualifies, which happens when a Special Ability or keyword grants an Evade Roll, or when the target is Engaged and suffering Damage from a Ranged Attack. The defender rolls all dice in the Damage Pool. Results equal to or higher than the Unit’s Evade characteristic are removed. Remaining dice are confirmed Damage.
5. **Resolve Damage and Casualties.** If there are no dice left, stop. Otherwise:
   - Multiply the remaining dice in the Damage Pool by the weapon’s Damage characteristic.
   - Add any Damage already recorded on the Unit by a Damage Marker, from this or earlier Rounds. This total is the Total Damage.
   - Apply any Special Abilities that reduce Total Damage.
   - Remove casualties model by model, following these rules:
     - The attack cannot remove more models than the number of Visible models in the target Unit.
     - While Total Damage equals or exceeds a model’s HP, remove one Visible model and reduce Total Damage by that model’s HP. Repeat.
     - If all Visible models are Destroyed, discard any remaining Total Damage. It does not carry over to non-Visible models.
     - If Total Damage remains but cannot Destroy the next Visible model, record it with a Damage Marker next to the Unit.

### 5.8 Removing Models from Engaged Units

When an Engaged Unit suffers casualties from any source, ignore the standard Visibility rules and remove models in this strict priority order:

1. Models not Within the Engagement Range of any Enemy Unit.
2. Models Within the Engagement Range but not in base-to-base contact with an Enemy model.
3. Models in base-to-base contact with an Enemy model.

The defending player cannot choose a model at Priority 2 or 3 if removing it would stop the Unit being Engaged with a specific Enemy Unit, provided another valid casualty exists in any tier.

### 5.9 Template Weapons

Blast Templates (BT) and Flamer Templates (FT) affect several models at once.

**Alignment.**

- BT: the template must cover as much of the target model’s base as possible. The centre of the target model’s base is the Target Point.
- FT: the narrow end must be flush with the attacking model’s base edge and aimed at the target. The centre of the target model’s base is the Target Point.
- Templates affect only models at the same elevation as the Target Point.
- Flying models are not on any elevation level for template purposes. A template set at Ground Level does not affect Flying models, and a template cannot be set on a Flying model’s elevation unless the weapon has Target: ALL or Target: Flying.

**Declaring targets.** Follow standard Target Eligibility (Section 5.5), with this exception: select one Enemy Unit as the Primary target. All models in that Unit covered by the template are the Main target. Every other model, Friendly or Enemy, covered by the template is resolved as Spillover, provided it shares the same elevation and Combat Tag as the Primary target.

**Determining hits.** A model is affected if all of these are true:

- Its base is partially or fully covered by the template.
- It is on the same elevation as the declared Target Point.
- A line traced from the target model’s base (BT) or the attacking model’s base (FT) to the model’s base is not blocked by terrain of Size 2 or larger.
- It is an eligible target type. For example, a Ground-only template does not affect Flying models, even if they are covered.

**Attack Pool.**

- Main target: generate Attack Dice equal to the number of affected models in the target Unit, plus any weapon Rate of Attack modifiers.
- Spillover: a separate Batch for each affected Unit. Generate Attack Dice equal to the number of affected models in that Unit only. Do not apply Rate of Attack modifiers. Do not apply Surge.

**Resolution.** Roll to Hit as normal. Successes move to the Armour Pool. For the Main target, the Surge Result equals the number of affected models in that Unit. Spillover does not generate Surge. Apply Surge efficiency normally to the Main target if the Combat Tag matches.

## 6. Phase 3: Combat

### 6.1 Activations

In this Phase, every Engaged Unit must activate. A player cannot choose to skip a fight.

- The First Player Marker holder chooses which player activates first. Players then alternate, each activating one Engaged Unit per turn.
- Only Engaged Units may be activated. Unengaged Units cannot act.
- Flying Units are never Engaged. They do not participate in the Combat Phase, cannot be activated, cannot Close Ranks, and cannot be the target of Close Combat Attacks.
- Once a Unit’s Close Combat Attack is fully resolved, remove its Activation Marker.

### 6.2 Passing

A player Passes only when they have no remaining Engaged Units to activate. When both players Pass, the Phase ends.

### 6.3 Close Combat Attack

An Engaged Ground Unit attacks the Enemy Units it is Engaged with.

**Step 1: Close Ranks (optional).** Before rolling, the Unit may press into the fight:

- Choose a Leading Model and move it up to 3” using standard Move rules, with two restrictions. The Leading Model must end closer to the Unit(s) it is Engaged with than it started. The move cannot bring the Unit into Engagement with new Enemy Units.
- Reposition the rest of the Unit into Coherency. Coherency Links may pass through Enemy models the Unit is Engaged with.
- Set models in base-to-base contact with Enemy models wherever possible. If that is not possible, set them in base-to-base contact with Friendly models from the same Unit that are within the Engagement Range.
- Models already in base-to-base contact at the start of this step are not moved.
- Models cannot use this move to Disengage from other Enemy Units.

**Step 2: Declare Attackers.**

- **Fighting Rank:** a model within the Engagement Range (1”) of an Enemy model.
- **Supporting Rank:** a model in base-to-base contact with a Friendly model from the same Unit that is in the Fighting Rank.

Line of Sight is not required. Eligible models strike the Enemy Unit as a whole.

**Step 3: Roll Attacks.** Resolve attacks using the same sequence as Ranged Attacks (Section 5.7), with these limits:

- Use only Combat Phase weapons listed on the Unit Card.
- If a model has more than one eligible Combat Phase weapon, its controlling player chooses one.
- Evade Rolls apply only if the Unit has a Special Ability or keyword that explicitly grants an Evade Roll against close combat attacks.

**Step 4: Fighting Multiple Units.** If the Unit is Engaged with more than one Enemy Unit:

- A model may attack an Enemy Unit only if it is in the Fighting Rank with a model from that Enemy Unit, or in the Supporting Rank of a model that is.
- Declare which target receives the Surge Die before rolling. The Surge Die cannot be split.

**Step 5: Casualty Removal.** Follow the rules in Section 5.8.

**Step 6: Post-Combat Status.** After casualties are removed:

- If none of the Unit’s remaining models is within 1” of an Enemy, the Unit is immediately Unengaged.
- A Unit freed from combat that has not yet activated this Phase is no longer bound by mandatory Combat activation. It passes, and acts normally from the next Round.

---

## 7. Engagement

A Ground Unit is Engaged when any of its models is within 1” (the Engagement Range) of any model in an Enemy Ground Unit, provided these conditions are met:

- **Combat Tags match.** Ground models engage only Ground models. Flying models cannot be Engaged by any model, and cannot engage other Flying models.
- **Terrain does not block.** Size 2 or larger terrain between the models prevents Engagement, even within 1”. Models on High Ground cannot engage models on Ground Level, and vice versa. Models on Mid Ground can engage models on High Ground or Ground Level only if both are adjacent to the same ACCESS POINT connecting their levels.

When any model in a Unit is Engaged, the whole Unit is Engaged. An Engaged Unit cannot perform a standard Move. It must Disengage or Hold.

Unless stated otherwise, a Unit cannot end any movement or be PLACEd within the Engagement Range of any Enemy model.

---

## 8. Phase 4: Scoring and Cleanup

The First Player Marker holder starts each step, and the steps run in this order:

1. Determine Mission Marker Control.
2. Score Victory Points.
3. End of Game Check.
4. Resolve End of the Round Effects.
5. Cleanup and Refresh.
6. Determine Initiative.

### 8.1 Determine Mission Marker Control

**Who may contest.** A Unit may contest a Mission Marker only if all three conditions are met:

- It is on the battlefield, not in Reserves.
- At least one model is Within 3”, has Line of Sight to the Marker, and is on the same elevation as the Marker. For Line of Sight, treat the Marker as Size 0.
- The Unit is In Coherency.

Flying Units and BURROWED Units cannot contest or control Mission Markers, whatever their position.

**Calculate control.** For each Marker:

- Each player adds up the Current Supply Value of all their contesting Units.
- The player with the higher total controls the Marker.
- A tie means the Marker is Contested, and control does not change.
- A Unit at Supply 0 may control a Marker if no Enemy Units contest it, but cannot win a contest.

**Sticky Control.** Set a Faction Indicator on each controlled Marker. Once a Marker is controlled, it stays under that player’s control, even if they move their Units away. Control passes only when the opponent takes it back by ending a Scoring Phase with a higher contesting Supply total. A Contested result never transfers control. A Marker cannot become neutral again once a player has controlled it.

### 8.2 Score Victory Points

Both players tally VPs at the same time, using the Mission Card’s scoring conditions.

### 8.3 End of Game Check

The game ends immediately if any of these is true:

- A Special Winning Condition is met. The player who meets it wins outright.
- A player has no models on the battlefield and no Units in Reserves. The surviving player gains +10 VP.
- The final Phase of the final Round is complete. Go to the Final Score (Section 9).

### 8.4 End of the Round Effects

Resolve all “End of the Round” abilities. The First Player Marker holder resolves theirs first. Each player with several such effects chooses their order, and each effect fully resolves before the next begins.

### 8.5 Cleanup and Refresh

- Remove all Tokens and Markers, except those with STAY IN PLAY, Damage Markers, Mission Markers, and Faction Indicators that show control of a Mission Marker.
- Flip all Exhausted Tactical and Faction Cards back to their Ready (face-up) side.

### 8.6 Determine Initiative

- The First Player Marker goes to the player with fewer Victory Points.
- If tied, the players roll off, and the winner takes the Marker.
- Begin Phase 1 of the next Round.

---

## 9. The Final Score

If the game ends at the Round limit:

- **Mission Score:** total the VP earned from objectives.
- **Final Round, All Units Destroyed:** at the start of the final Scoring Phase, all Units still in Reserves are treated as Destroyed. Each player is awarded VP for these Units as the Mission Card’s scoring conditions for Destroyed Enemy Units require.
- **Winner:** the player with the highest final VP total wins.
- **Tiebreaker:** if totals are equal, apply the Mission Card’s tiebreaker. If none is defined, the game is a Draw.

## 11. Turn Checklist

**Each Round**

1. Resolve Start of the Round abilities. Update the Supply Pool.
2. Phase 1: Movement. Activate Units alternately until both players pass.
3. Phase 2: Assault. Activate Units alternately until both players pass.
4. Phase 3: Combat. Every Engaged Unit activates. Players pass when none remain.
5. Phase 4: Scoring and Cleanup. Control, score, end-of-game check, end-of-round effects, cleanup, initiative.

**Each Activation**

1. Choose an eligible Unit and an action allowed in this Phase.
2. Use the action’s rules. Check Coherency, Engagement, and Line of Sight as they apply.
3. Set the Activation Marker for this Phase.
